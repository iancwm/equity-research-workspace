# Economic Engine — Apple

**Assessment:** Cash compounder, with product-led installed-base growth and an increasingly important high-margin Services layer. [OBS-002, OBS-005, OBS-006, CON-001]

## How the business makes money

Apple sells premium hardware (iPhone, Mac, iPad, Wearables/Home/Accessories) and monetizes its ecosystem through Services. In FY2025, iPhone was $209.6B of sales and Services was $109.2B. Services was only 26.2% of revenue but 42.2% of gross profit because its 75.4% gross margin materially exceeded products' 36.8%. [OBS-004, OBS-005, DER-002]

```text
Active device base → upgrade/retention + new-device demand → hardware revenue
Active device base × Services ARPU → Services revenue
(Product revenue × product GM) + (Services revenue × Services GM) − R&D − SG&A → EBIT
EBIT − cash tax + non-cash charges − capex − working-capital investment → FCF
```

The installed base cannot be independently quantified from the cited disclosures, but management says it reached an all-time high in FQ3 2026. It is therefore treated as a qualitative KPI, not a model input. [S-002]

## Primary earnings drivers

| Driver | Mechanism | Current evidence | Monitor |
|---|---|---|---|
| iPhone demand, mix and ASP | iPhone remains the largest revenue pool; premium mix lifts revenue and product GM | FQ3 iPhone sales rose 22% YoY, primarily Pro models | iPhone revenue and product gross margin |
| Services ARPU and mix | Higher-margin recurring/transactional revenues lift consolidated GM | FQ3 Services grew 12%; FY2025 Services GM was 75.4% | Services growth versus company growth |
| Component costs | Memory, NAND and DRAM raise hardware COGS | Company says cost pressure is rising and may intensify | Adjusted gross margin |
| Product-cycle timing | Launch timing affects channel fill and customer deferral | Company identifies seasonality and product introductions as material | September-quarter revenue and channel commentary |
| Buybacks | Lower share count increases EPS even if net income growth slows | $61.8B repurchased in FY2026's first nine months | Diluted shares and repurchase pace |

[OBS-002, OBS-007, OBS-009]

## Cost structure and cash conversion

Hardware COGS are predominantly variable with units, mix, components, logistics and tariffs. R&D is the main semi-fixed strategic expense; it was 8% of FY2025 revenue. SG&A mixes selling-variable and corporate costs. Apple generated $111.5B of FY2025 operating cash flow against $12.7B of capex, or $98.8B of simple FCF, indicating low physical-capital intensity relative to its revenue base. [OBS-006, S-003]

FY2026 reported gross margin is not a clean through-cycle base: FQ3's 50.1% included about 200 bp from tariff refunds. The base model therefore uses 48.3% FY2026 GM and a 32.1% FY2027 EBIT margin rather than extrapolating the FQ3 reported figure. [OBS-003, DER-001, A-002]

## Sensitivities

1. **iPhone premium mix / revenue:** iPhone's scale makes a change in upgrade demand or ASP the largest near-term sales sensitivity.
2. **Component inflation versus pricing:** memory costs are the key direct gross-margin sensitivity; premium positioning and scale may help, but do not immunize Apple.
3. **Services growth and take rate:** Services mix can compound consolidated margin, while regulatory change can reduce the economic capture of some digital transactions.

## Falsification and gaps

The cash-compounder view weakens if FQ4 adjusted gross margin is below 46%, if it remains below 46.5% in FQ1, or if Services grows more slowly than total revenue. These thresholds align with the normalized FQ4 guide of 46%-47% adjusted gross margin. [DER-004, F-002] No independently sourced active-device count, Services ARPU, or segment operating income is available in this research state; do not make a more granular unit/ARPU attribution without further sourcing.
