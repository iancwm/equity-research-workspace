# Value Chain & Pricing Power — Apple

## Where value accrues

Apple sits between semiconductor/component suppliers, contract manufacturers, distribution/carrier partners, developers/content partners and end users. Its economic rents arise from integrated hardware/software, brand, ecosystem switching costs, distribution scale and the monetization of an installed base. The clearest disclosed financial evidence is the 75.4% FY2025 Services gross margin versus 36.8% for products. [OBS-005]

## Pricing-power stress test

| Test | Evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Price above input inflation | Apple says price actions may not fully mitigate component costs and could reduce demand | Partial; not proven [OBS-009] |
| Preserve demand in an industry downturn | Apple gained share as Q2 global smartphone units fell 7.4% | Constructive but only one quarter of third-party evidence [OBS-010] |
| Capture service economics | Services' 75.4% FY2025 gross margin indicates substantial ecosystem value capture | Strong, subject to regulatory constraints [OBS-005] |
| Retain distribution control | EU DMA creates alternative stores, web distribution and alternative payments | Weaker in EU, with financial effect undisclosed [OBS-011] |

## Supplier and customer balance

Apple's scale, long-term purchasing ability and premium mix are likely negotiating advantages during component stress. IDC's evidence that the crisis rewards scale and premium mix supports this inference. Conversely, the company explicitly identifies advanced semiconductors, NAND and DRAM as constrained and says costs may rise further; scale reduces relative disadvantage, not the absolute cost base. [OBS-009, OBS-010]

On the customer side, Apple has product/ecosystem switching costs, but device purchases are discretionary and product price increases can lower demand. Developer bargaining power has increased in the EU because the DMA permits alternate distribution and payment routes. Apple’s new EU terms retain commissions, but this research does not quantify the affected revenue pool. [OBS-011, R-002]

## Conclusion

Apple has durable but not unlimited pricing power. The business can likely protect relative premium economics better than lower-end OEMs; it cannot be assumed to pass through every memory or tariff shock without volume or mix consequences. The moat is structural, while the present cost shock is cyclical. [CON-002]
