## 1. Investment Thesis and Executive Summary

Grab Holdings is the dominant Southeast Asian superapp, combining ride-hailing (Mobility), food and grocery delivery (Deliveries), and digital financial services (payments, lending, digital banking) across eight countries and roughly 900 cities. After thirteen years of cash-burning growth — including a $1,740m net loss as recently as FY2022 [RG-01] — Grab delivered its **first full year of net profit in FY2025** ($200m) and has now posted **eighteen consecutive quarters of Adjusted EBITDA growth** through Q2 2026, with management raising full-year 2026 guidance at the most recent print [CO-01, CO-02].

The investment debate is not whether Grab's core marketplace economics have improved — they plainly have, with Group Adjusted EBITDA growing from -$22m (FY2023) to $500m (FY2025), a run rate now guided toward $720-740m in FY2026 [EE-04, CO-02] — but whether that improvement is (a) durable against a still-underpenetrated but re-arming competitive field in food delivery, and (b) sufficient to justify closing the valuation gap to global and regional peers. At ~9.6x forward EV/Adjusted EBITDA, Grab trades at a meaningful discount to Uber (~14.8x) and to its most direct regional comparable, GoTo (~15.1x), despite comparable-or-superior forecast EBITDA growth and structurally stronger segment economics than GoTo specifically [VAL-04, VAL-05].

**Three things that matter most:**

1. **Deliveries margin durability.** Deliveries Segment Adjusted EBITDA margin has expanded from an implied ~1.65% of GMV (FY2024) to 2.0% (FY2025) to 2.3% (Q2 2026), driven substantially by advertising monetization rather than commission increases [EE-08, VC-04]. This is the single largest driver of valuation dispersion in this analysis — a 100bp swing is worth roughly $200-240m of Group Adjusted EBITDA by FY2028E [VAL-09] — and it has not yet been tested against a well-funded competitive response.
2. **Financial Services' unresolved path to profitability.** The segment lost $110m at the Adjusted EBITDA line in FY2025 (widening 5% YoY) even as its loan book grew 120-197% YoY [EE-05, RISK-04], and GXS Group's independently reported S$208m FY2025 loss does not cleanly reconcile to Grab's consolidated segment figure — an open contradiction, not a resolved one [C-04].
3. **A live regulatory test in Vietnam.** Vietnam's National Competition Commission opened a review of Grab's fare-setting, commission, and fee/deduction policies in September 2026 after drivers called for a work stoppage [VC-05, S008] — the first concrete regulatory test of Grab's pricing power in a major market, with potential to be referenced elsewhere in ASEAN.

**Rating rationale.** We initiate with an **Overweight** rating. The base case (Section 7) implies 41% upside over roughly two years, built on management's own disclosed FY2028 targets rather than aggressive extrapolation, and the current price appears to already embed meaningful skepticism about competitive and execution risk (Section 7.6) rather than pricing a best-case outcome. However, the bear case (-33%) is real and not merely a tail scenario — this is a constructive, not maximum-conviction, call, and position sizing should reflect the genuine two-sided risk documented throughout this report.

## 2. Company Overview and Business Model

Founded in 2012 and headquartered in Singapore, Grab began as a ride-hailing app and has expanded into a three-segment superapp serving Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, and Vietnam. It listed on Nasdaq via SPAC merger in December 2021 and reports in US dollars under IFRS, despite earning and spending almost entirely in Southeast Asian local currencies [MT-04].

**Segments (FY2025 revenue mix: Deliveries 53%, Mobility 36%, Financial Services 10%, Others <1%) [EE-02]:**

- **Deliveries** — food and grocery delivery, parcel delivery, and Grab's owned grocery retail footprint in Malaysia (Jaya Grocer, Everrise). Increasingly, this segment also monetizes a **self-serve advertising marketplace** sold to merchant-partners, which management explicitly credits for recent margin gains [EE-08]. FY2025 revenue $1,800m (+21% YoY), GMV $14,236m (+21% YoY), Segment Adjusted EBITDA $287m (2.0% of GMV) [EE-02, EE-03, EE-05].
- **Mobility** — ride-hailing across cars, two-wheelers, and taxis, plus vehicle rental (GrabRentals). This is Grab's profit engine: FY2025 revenue $1,219m (+16% YoY), GMV $7,901m (+19% YoY), Segment Adjusted EBITDA $690m (8.7% of GMV) — more than double Deliveries' segment profit despite roughly half the GMV [EE-02, EE-03, EE-05].
- **Financial Services** — GrabPay payments, consumer/merchant lending, digital banking (GXS Bank in Singapore, GXBank in Malaysia, and the newly consolidating SuperBank and Stash in Indonesia), and insurance distribution. FY2025 revenue $347m (+37% YoY), loan portfolio $1,180m net / $1,278m gross (+120% YoY), Segment Adjusted EBITDA -$110m (loss widened 5% YoY on credit-loss provisioning) [EE-02, EE-05]. Customer deposits across GXS Singapore and GXBank Malaysia reached $1.6bn at end-2025 [S002].

**Business model logic.** Grab's core advantage is a shared distribution surface: a single app through which 47-54 million monthly transacting users [CO-01, EE-03] access rides, deliveries, payments, and increasingly lending and banking products. This bundling lowers customer-acquisition cost for newer products (Financial Services) and, more importantly, has begun generating a genuinely high-incremental-margin revenue stream in advertising, which monetizes existing attention and GMV without incremental partner-incentive spend [EE-09]. The model is best understood as **two mature, differently-monetizing marketplaces (Mobility, increasingly Deliveries) funding one capital-intensive optionality bet (Financial Services)** — see Section 4 for the balance-sheet and capital-allocation implications of that structure.

## 3. Industry, Value Chain, and Competitive Landscape

Southeast Asia's on-demand economy continues to grow at 19-21% GMV rates [EE-03], well above developed-market comparables, reflecting continued smartphone/digital-payments penetration and urbanization. Within that growth, competitive intensity varies sharply by segment and country.

**Food delivery.** Grab holds an estimated ~54-55% share of Southeast Asian food-delivery GMV, the clear regional leader. Foodpanda (Delivery Hero) is a distant #2 regionally (~11%) but a much closer #2 in Singapore (24%) and Malaysia (22%); ShopeeFood (Sea Limited) holds ~9-12%; Gojek (GoTo) holds ~10% [VC-01, S009, S010]. Deliveroo's exit from Singapore in March 2026 vacated roughly 7% share, a modest near-term tailwind for Grab and Foodpanda [VC-03].

**Ride-hailing.** Grab is dominant in Malaysia (>95% share) and holds an estimated ~70% of combined Southeast Asian Mobility GMV, but GoTo/Gojek retains an estimated majority (~60%) of Indonesian ride-hailing [VC-02] — Grab's single largest population market and the one country where its competitive position is genuinely contested rather than commanding. This is precisely the market a Grab-GoTo combination would resolve (see Section 8).

**Competitive structure and moat.** Grab's advantage rests on two-sided network density (driver supply attracts riders/orders; volume attracts more drivers/merchants), reinforced by superapp bundling. Switching costs are real but moderate — Southeast Asian consumers are well-documented multi-homers across Grab, Foodpanda, and GoTo — which caps absolute pricing power relative to a true single-app-lock-in business [VC section 2]. Regulatory/licensing barriers provide more durable protection in Mobility than in Deliveries, which is the structural reason Mobility margins (8.7% of GMV) run roughly 4x Deliveries margins (2.0% of GMV) [EE-05].

**Pricing-power stress test.** FY2025 provided a genuine, if provisional, positive signal: Grab expanded both Deliveries and Mobility segment margins *simultaneously* with 19-21% GMV growth — margin gains did not require volume or share sacrifice [VC-04]. But this has not yet been tested against a well-funded, motivated competitive response. Maybank's scenario work estimates that "moderate" competitive intensity could still cut 2030 Deliveries EBITDA by ~23% versus base case, and "aggressive" competition (e.g., a ShopeeFood- or Meituan-backed Foodpanda) could cut it by ~41% [S010]. Where margin compression would land if competition re-intensified: primarily on **Grab's and Foodpanda's own P&Ls** via partner/consumer incentives — not on restaurant merchants or consumers — based on FY2025 behavior, where incentive spend grew roughly in line with GMV even in a relatively benign competitive year [VC-06].

**The key structural swing factor: Foodpanda's ownership.** Delivery Hero has signaled willingness to exit Southeast Asia. A ShopeeFood (Sea Limited) acquisition of Foodpanda would combine it with Sea's e-commerce/payments/logistics ecosystem into a far stronger #2; a Meituan acquisition would import an operator with much deeper food-delivery execution experience than either current player [VC-03]. Neither outcome has crystallized as of this writing, and this workspace treats it as an open, monitorable variable rather than a base-case assumption.

**The labor/regulatory dimension.** Grab classifies driver-partners as independent contractors across ASEAN. Vietnam's National Competition Commission opened a review of Grab's fare-setting, commission, and fee/deduction policies in September 2026, directed at Grab after drivers called for a weekend work stoppage over falling take-home pay [VC-05, S008]. This is the most concrete, live test to date of whether Grab's commission structure — effectively its "cost of goods" for driver-partner supply — is durable against regulatory intervention, and Vietnam has historically served as a bellwether for broader ASEAN gig-economy policy.

## 4. Asset Portfolio, Operating Exposure, and Strategic Position

Grab's balance sheet (as of Dec 31, 2025) shows total assets of $11,983m against total liabilities of $5,226m and total equity of $6,757m [CO-03]. The asset base splits into two very different risk profiles:

- **The on-demand platform (Mobility, most of Deliveries)** is genuinely asset-light: capex was only $188m in FY2025 (5.6% of revenue), concentrated in technology infrastructure and the Jaya Grocer/Everrise store footprint [Module 1, Section 9]. Property, plant and equipment on the balance sheet was $831m at end-2025, alongside $1,057m of intangible assets and goodwill (accumulated M&A) [S002].
- **The financial-services build-out** is increasingly capital-intensive: loan receivables (current + non-current) totaled $1,180m at end-2025, funded partly by $1,629m of customer deposits from the banking business and partly by Grab's own balance sheet. This book has grown 120-197% YoY across the periods observed [EE-05, RISK-04] — a genuine and growing capital commitment that sits partly outside the clean, asset-light on-demand narrative.

**Capital structure.** Grab issued $1.5bn of convertible notes in FY2025, a USD-denominated liability against a predominantly local-currency-earning asset base — a deliberate, modest financial-currency mismatch layered on top of the natural operating FX hedge in the core business [MT-01, MT-04]. Gross cash liquidity stood at $7.4bn and net cash liquidity (gross cash less loans and borrowings) at $5.4bn as of both end-2025 and Q2 2026 [CO-03, CO-01] — a large net-cash position that funds both the ongoing Financial Services build and an expanding shareholder-return program (cumulative buyback authorization raised from an initial $500m to $1.75bn as of Q2 2026 [S001, S002]).

**Governance.** Grab maintains a dual-class share structure (3,974m Class A / 105m Class B ordinary shares as of Q2 2026 [CO-04]), concentrating voting influence with insiders/founders. This is relevant to how much minority shareholders can influence major capital-allocation or strategic decisions — including, notably, any eventual Grab-GoTo combination [RISK-03].

**Strategic position.** Grab holds the most complete superapp footprint in Southeast Asia and the most credible near-term path among regional peers to combining category leadership (Deliveries, most of Mobility) with a genuinely optionality-rich, if still lossmaking, financial-services build. Its principal strategic vulnerability is the mirror image of its strength: the same superapp breadth that provides distribution advantages also means Grab is fighting on more fronts (food delivery competitors, ride-hailing competitors, and now digital-bank competitors) than a single-vertical peer, with a shareholder base (including Uber's ~13.5% stake, a legacy of Uber's 2018 Southeast Asia exit) that is not fully aligned around the same time horizon [RISK-02].

**Reading the trend.** Three things stand out. First, the **acceleration in Adjusted EBITDA relative to revenue** — FY2025 revenue grew 20% while Adjusted EBITDA grew 60%, and this pattern continued into Q2 2026 (revenue +22%, Adjusted EBITDA +54%) — is clear evidence of positive operating leverage, not merely a one-off cost cut [Module 1, Section 8]. Second, **net cash from operating activities is a poor proxy for underlying cash generation** for this business: it fell from $852m (FY2024) to just $79m (FY2025), a swing driven almost entirely by working-capital movements in the lending and digital-banking business (higher loan disbursements, smaller relative deposit growth), not by any deterioration in the core platform — which is precisely why Grab (and this analysis) relies on **Adjusted Free Cash Flow**, which rose from $162m to $290m over the same period, as the decision-useful cash metric [EE-05 discussion in Module 1, Section 5; CO-09]. Third, **reported net income is flattered by non-operating items**: FY2025's $200m net profit included $203m of net finance income (mostly interest on Grab's large cash balance, plus a $34m non-cash fair-value gain on the convertible-note derivative) against only $65m of IFRS operating profit [CO-09, MT-01, MT-02] — a rate-sensitive, partly non-cash contribution that will not scale one-for-one with operating performance.

**Segment trend detail (FY2024 → FY2025):** Deliveries Segment Adjusted EBITDA nearly doubled ($196m → $287m, margin +35bps to 2.0% of GMV); Mobility grew a more moderate 21% ($569m → $690m, margin +10bps to 8.7%); Financial Services losses widened slightly (-$105m → -$110m) despite the loan book more than doubling, which this analysis reads as consistent with a deliberate, provisioning-driven build phase rather than a deteriorating unit-economics signal — though this remains an open question given limited disclosure (see Section 8 and CS-04).

**Key assumptions and their rationale (full detail and bear/bull ranges in `state/assumptions.json`):**

- **A-01/A-02**: FY2026E revenue and Adjusted EBITDA set at the midpoint of Grab's own raised guidance ($4.10-4.15bn revenue; $720-740m Adjusted EBITDA), issued at the Q2 2026 print and explicitly citing SuperBank/Stash consolidation as a contributor alongside organic momentum.

- **A-06/A-07/A-08**: segment-level margin paths (Deliveries reaching ~3.0% of GMV, Mobility ~9.5%, Financial Services narrowing to roughly -$40m by FY2028E in the base case) are the underlying drivers that, summed, are consistent with the $1.5bn Group target.

- **A-10**: diluted share count is assumed to decline modestly from FY2025's 4,206m (weighted-average diluted) toward ~4,050m by FY2028E as the expanded $1.75bn buyback authorization is deployed, partially offset by convertible-note dilution and continued (though likely moderating) stock-based compensation issuance.

**The bear and bull cases (FY2028E)** vary the same drivers rather than introducing new ones: the bear case assumes Deliveries margin stalls near 1.5% of GMV (renewed competition), Financial Services losses widen to -$150m (credit-cycle deterioration), and revenue growth decelerates faster (8-10% p.a. in the outer years), yielding FY2028E Adjusted EBITDA of only $1,000m — a ~33% shortfall to the management target. The bull case assumes Deliveries margin reaches 4.0% of GMV, Financial Services reaches breakeven-to-modest-profit, and growth holds in the high-teens, yielding $1,600m — modestly above the management target.

**EBIT and net-income reconciliation caveat.** The EBIT and net-income lines above are derived bridges (assuming the historical Adjusted-EBITDA-to-EBIT gap — D&A, share-based compensation, restructuring/M&A costs, ~12.9% of revenue in FY2025 — gradually narrows toward ~11% of revenue as SBC normalizes with scale), not company-guided figures. They are decision-useful for order-of-magnitude EPS and FCF framing but carry materially lower confidence than the revenue/Adjusted EBITDA lines, which trace directly to company guidance [CO-06, CO-07, CO-08].

This is the one metric that should dominate ongoing monitoring of the thesis (see Section 8 and `outputs/dashboard.md`).

### 7.6 Expectations classification

**Reasonable-to-Depressed, not Demanding.** The current price does not appear to assume Grab's profitability streak, guidance-raise pattern, and 2028 targets will be delivered without friction — it already embeds real (though not catastrophic) skepticism. This creates asymmetry that favors continued position-building on confirming evidence, but it is not proof of mispricing on its own [VAL-10].

## 8. Catalysts, Risks, Debate Map, and Decision Rules

### 8.1 Catalysts (12-24 months)

- **CAT-01**: Continued execution against raised FY2026 guidance at the remaining Q3/Q4 2026 prints — a beat-and-raise pattern would reinforce the profitability narrative and could compress the valuation gap to peers.
- **CAT-02**: Resolution (in either direction) of Grab-GoTo merger speculation — a combination would materially reduce Indonesian competitive intensity in Grab's largest underweight market; a credible, durable denial would remove an M&A-integration-risk overhang some investors may be pricing.
- **CO-10**: SuperBank/Stash consolidation delivering disclosed Financial Services revenue/loan-book growth materially above the FY2025 37% YoY pace, validating the guidance-raise rationale.
- Resolution of Foodpanda's ownership situation in a way that preserves rather than intensifies competitive discipline (Section 3).

### 8.2 Risks

- **RISK-01 / VC-05**: Driver/partner classification and gig-economy labor regulation, now concretely live via the Vietnam National Competition Commission review of commission/fare policies opened September 2026 — the most immediate, dated risk in this report.
- **RISK-02**: Uber's estimated ~13.5% stake in Grab is a persistent secondary-sale overhang, independent of operating fundamentals.
- **RISK-03**: Dual-class governance concentrates strategic decision-making (including any GoTo combination) with insiders, limiting minority-shareholder influence.
- **RISK-04**: Credit risk in the rapidly scaling loan book (gross loan portfolio +197% YoY as of Q2 2026); even NPLs "well within risk appetite" today can deteriorate quickly on a book growing this fast if regional consumer credit conditions weaken — and this cannot be independently verified from current disclosure (CS-04).
- Renewed food-delivery price competition (Section 3), the single largest quantified swing factor in this analysis.

### 8.4 Decision rules / falsification criteria

- **The thesis strengthens** if Deliveries Segment Adjusted EBITDA margin continues expanding through at least two more quarters (toward or above 2.5% of GMV) without GMV deceleration — this would support the "Depressed" end of the valuation-expectations classification and argue for adding to the position [VAL-10, DASH-03].
- **The thesis weakens** if Deliveries margin falls back below 2.0% of GMV or GMV growth decelerates below 15% YoY constant currency, signaling the competitive re-intensification Maybank's bear scenario contemplates [DASH-05].
- **The thesis is falsified in its macro/rate-sensitivity dimension** if net finance income declines materially faster than the net-cash-liquidity trend implies, or if the reported-vs-constant-currency growth spread turns persistently and materially negative beyond the 3-4pp already flagged for Q3 2026 [MT-06].
- **A confirmed adverse Vietnam regulatory ruling that is referenced or replicated in Indonesia, Philippines, or Thailand** would be treated as a material, thesis-changing development requiring immediate re-underwriting of the value-chain/pricing-power module [DASH-04].

## 9. Conclusion, Rating, and What Would Change the View

Grab has crossed a genuine inflection point: eighteen consecutive quarters of Adjusted EBITDA growth, a first full year of Group net profit, and a management team that has now raised guidance rather than defended it at the last several quarterly prints. The core on-demand business (Mobility structurally, Deliveries provisionally) shows real operating leverage and — for now — the ability to expand margins without sacrificing growth. Financial Services remains a genuine, only partially transparent capital commitment whose ultimate returns are not yet provable from public disclosure.

At current levels, the stock appears to price meaningful skepticism about the durability of these gains rather than crediting management's own targets outright — a configuration that favors continued exposure while the two highest-information-value monitorables (Deliveries segment margin, and the Vietnam regulatory outcome) play out over the next one to two quarters.

**Rating: Overweight.** Base-case price target $4.29 (+41% from $3.05), against a bear case of $2.03 (-33%) and a bull case of $5.99 (+96%). This is a constructive, evidence-based call built on management's own disclosed targets and a peer-relative valuation gap that is only partially explained by fundamentals — not a high-conviction, low-risk recommendation. The bear case is real: renewed Deliveries competition, an adverse Vietnam (or broader ASEAN) regulatory outcome, or a Financial Services credit-cycle deterioration could each independently impair the thesis.

**One-paragraph thesis.** Grab sits at a mid-cycle profitability inflection within a still-structurally-growing Southeast Asian on-demand market: earnings direction is positive (guidance raised, not cut, at the last print), but valuation already reflects meaningful doubt about whether Deliveries' recent, advertising-led margin gains and the still-lossmaking Financial Services build will hold up against a resurgent Foodpanda/ShopeeFood/Meituan competitive threat and newly concrete ASEAN gig-economy regulatory scrutiny; the main catalyst is continued quarterly evidence that Deliveries margin expansion survives competitive stress, the main risk is that it doesn't (or that Vietnam's regulatory review is replicated regionally), and the single most important number to watch next is the Deliveries Segment Adjusted EBITDA margin printed at the Q3 2026 result.

**What would most change this view:** (1) a confirmed, multi-quarter reversal in Deliveries segment margin; (2) a binding Vietnam (or broader ASEAN) regulatory ruling that caps commissions or forces fare-setting changes; (3) credible evidence that Financial Services credit losses are scaling faster than loan-book growth would explain; or (4) a definitive Grab-GoTo corporate action in either direction, which would require a full re-underwriting of the Indonesian competitive-landscape assumptions in Section 3.
