# Module 1 — Economic Engine: How Grab Actually Makes Money

_Ledger references: EE-01 to EE-09. Sources: S001, S002, S003._

## 1. Industry architecture

Grab operates a three-segment "superapp" model layered over a common driver/merchant/consumer network across eight Southeast Asian countries (Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam; ~900 cities):

- **Deliveries** — food and grocery delivery (including owned grocery retail under Jaya Grocer and Everrise in Malaysia), parcel delivery, and an increasingly important **self-serve advertising marketplace** sold to merchant-partners.
- **Mobility** — ride-hailing (cars, two-wheelers, taxis) and vehicle rental (GrabRentals).
- **Financial Services** — payments (GrabPay), consumer and merchant lending, digital banking (GXS Bank in Singapore, GXBank in Malaysia, and the newly consolidating SuperBank/Stash in Indonesia), and insurance distribution.

Revenue pools concentrate unevenly: Deliveries is the largest revenue segment ($1,800m FY2025, 53% of Group revenue) and largest GMV pool ($14.2bn), but Mobility is the profit engine (segment Adjusted EBITDA of $690m FY2025 vs Deliveries' $287m, despite Mobility GMV being roughly half of Deliveries GMV). Financial Services is the smallest revenue contributor (10% of Group revenue) and the only segment still losing money at the segment level (EE-02, EE-05).

## 2. The value chain map

**Inputs** (driver-partners' vehicles/labor, merchant-partners' kitchens/inventory, Grab's own capital for the loan book) → **Production/Delivery** (Grab's matching, routing, and payments technology stack; Grab-owned grocery stores for Jaya Grocer/Everrise) → **Distribution** (the Grab app, a single distribution surface across all three segments) → **Customer** (54 million monthly transacting users, Q2 2026) → **Recurring/Aftermarket Revenue** (advertising sold against repeat merchant exposure; lending and deposit relationships cross-sold to the existing MTU base, which is Grab's structural distribution advantage over standalone fintech or delivery competitors).

## 3. Where revenue, gross profit and rents concentrate

- **Revenue** concentrates in Deliveries GMV monetization (take rate plus advertising) and in Mobility fares.
- **Segment profit** concentrates in Mobility: an 8.7% EBITDA/GMV margin (FY2025) is structurally protected by two-sided network effects, driver-supply relationships, and — in several markets — licensing/regulatory barriers that limit new entrants (EE-05).
- **Economic rents** are increasingly captured through **advertising**, which monetizes existing GMV and user attention without incremental partner-incentive spend, and is explicitly credited by management for Deliveries' recent margin gains (EE-08, EE-09).
- **Financial Services** does not yet generate rents; it is a capital-consuming build phase (loan book +120-197% YoY) whose eventual profit pool (net interest margin plus fee income) is not yet realized (EE-05, CS-03).

## 4. Company-specific KPIs that mathematically drive profitability

1. **On-Demand GMV** ($22.1bn FY2025) — the volume base for both Deliveries and Mobility revenue.
2. **Take rate** (revenue / GMV, ~15.2% blended, EE-07) — how much of GMV Grab converts to revenue after partner/consumer incentives.
3. **Segment Adjusted EBITDA margin (% of GMV)** — Deliveries 2.0%, Mobility 8.7% (FY2025) — the key profitability differentiator between segments.
4. **Monthly Transacting Users (MTUs)** and **GMV per MTU** — 47.2m MTUs and $513 GMV/MTU (FY2025) — frequency/engagement drivers.
5. **Partner and consumer incentives** (as % of GMV) — the primary lever competitors and Grab itself pull to defend or gain share; a direct drag on take rate.
6. **Loan portfolio size and credit-loss provisioning rate** — drives Financial Services' path to breakeven.
7. **Regional corporate costs** ($368m FY2025, allocated outside segments) — the shared-services cost base that must scale sub-linearly to revenue for Group Adjusted EBITDA margin to expand.

## 5. Simplified equations

- **Revenue ≈ On-Demand GMV × Blended Take Rate + Financial Services Revenue + Advertising Revenue** (advertising embedded within Deliveries revenue, not separately disclosed).
- **Group Adjusted EBITDA = Σ(Segment Adjusted EBITDA) − Regional Corporate Costs** = $868m − $368m = $500m (FY2025, ties to EE-04/EE-05).
- **Adjusted FCF ≈ Net Cash from Operating Activities − Capex + PP&E disposal proceeds − Δ(loan receivables) − Δ(customer deposits)**, i.e., Grab explicitly strips out the working-capital swings created by the lending and digital-banking business to isolate the "core" cash-generative platform (per Grab's own non-IFRS definition, S002). This is the correct metric to track for platform cash generation; **unadjusted net cash from operating activities is not** (FY2025 $79m vs FY2024 $852m — a swing driven almost entirely by loan-book/deposit growth, not underlying deterioration; see CO-09 and RG-04).

## 6. Variables with greatest earnings sensitivity

1. **Deliveries Segment Adjusted EBITDA margin (% of GMV)** — the single largest source of scenario dispersion in this workspace (VAL-09): a 100bp swing is worth roughly $200-240m of Group Adjusted EBITDA on an estimated ~$20-24bn FY2028E Deliveries GMV base.
2. **Financial Services credit-loss provisioning rate** relative to loan-book growth — determines whether the segment's loss narrows or widens as the book scales 120%+ YoY.
3. **Regional corporate cost operating leverage** — Grab has begun showing this (costs improved as % of revenue YoY at Q4 2025 despite absolute dollar growth), but sustained leverage here compounds directly into Group EBITDA margin.

## 7. Cost structure

- **Fixed/semi-fixed**: regional corporate costs (technology infrastructure, mapping/payment platform R&D, corporate G&A) — largely fixed in the near term, providing operating leverage as GMV scales.
- **Variable/pass-through**: partner incentives (driver/merchant payments) and consumer incentives — the primary variable cost lever, directly tied to competitive intensity (VC-06).
- **Commodity-linked**: fuel costs are borne principally by driver-partners, not Grab directly, but transmit indirectly into partner-incentive requirements when fuel prices rise (MT-05) — an important, easily overlooked commodity linkage.
- **Credit-linked**: net impairment losses on financial assets ($140m FY2025, +47% YoY) scale with the loan book and are a real, cash-relevant cost embedded in operating profit (not added back to Adjusted EBITDA).

## 8. Operating leverage and incremental margins

Group revenue grew 20% YoY in FY2025 while Adjusted EBITDA grew 60% YoY — a clear demonstration of positive operating leverage, driven primarily by (a) Mobility's already-high incremental margin, (b) Deliveries' advertising mix shift, and (c) regional corporate costs growing slower (5% YoY, $350m→$368m) than revenue (20% YoY). This pattern continued into Q2 2026 (revenue +22%, Adjusted EBITDA +54%).

## 9. Capital intensity and cash conversion

Grab is asset-light in Mobility/Deliveries (capex was only 5.6% of revenue in FY2025, $188m, mostly technology infrastructure and Jaya Grocer/Everrise store buildout) but increasingly capital-intensive in Financial Services, where the loan book itself is a growing balance-sheet commitment (net loan receivables $1,180m at Dec-2025, gross $2,318m at Jun-2026) funded by a mix of customer deposits ($1,629m) and Grab's own balance sheet. Incremental ROIC in the core on-demand business appears high (EBITDA growing faster than the asset base), but blended Group ROIC is not yet meaningful given the accumulated deficit (-$17.5bn) still on the balance sheet from Grab's pre-IPO cash-burn years.

## 10. Mature-model classification

**Cash Compounder in transition, with an embedded Capital Cycle sub-business.** Mobility already resembles a cash-compounding, high-return marketplace business. Deliveries is transitioning from a subsidized-growth model toward a cash-compounding model as advertising scales. Financial Services remains in an explicit **Capital Cycle** build phase (reinvesting for scale, accepting losses, targeting future returns) that could migrate to Cash Compounder status if credit performance holds (CS-03), or could reveal itself as **Structural Cash Burn** if loan losses outpace revenue generation (CS-04, RISK-04) — this is the single largest unresolved classification question in the economic-engine analysis.

## Research gaps and falsification

- RG-01: FY2022 figures are secondary-sourced only (low confidence).
- RG-04: Non-IFRS Adjusted EBITDA reconciliation methodology changed in FY2025 (M&A costs reclassified); pre-FY2024 figures are only approximately comparable.
- **Falsification of the "Cash Compounder in transition" conclusion**: would be undermined if Deliveries Adjusted EBITDA margin reverses below FY2024 levels (1.65%) for two consecutive quarters, or if Financial Services losses widen faster than loan-book growth would explain (i.e., provisioning rate rising, not just provisioning dollars).
