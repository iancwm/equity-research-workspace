# Sector primer workflow

Use the smallest mode that satisfies the request. A sector primer should educate first and support decisions second; do not jump to valuation conclusions before explaining the operating system of the industry.

## Mode selection

- **Quick primer** is appropriate for an initial orientation, a meeting brief, or a request explicitly avoiding persistence. Build a provisional scope, explain the value chain and drivers, show only a compact data/comps snapshot, and mark hypotheses as provisional.
- **Full primer** is appropriate when the reader needs a reusable research product or when the sector has no reliable existing state. Initialize or use a dedicated sector workspace and complete the sequence below.
- **Update** is appropriate for new industry data, regulation, technology, prices, earnings, peer membership, or market valuations. Preserve the prior snapshot, supersede changed records, identify stale downstream sections, and recompute only affected analytics and synthesis.
- **Publish/edit** is appropriate when validated state exists and the task is formatting, tightening, or rendering. Do not quietly add new sector facts during an editing pass.

## Full-primer sequence

### 1. Establish the frame

Record the sector definition and classification, geography, subsectors, value-chain perimeter, listed-company universe, currency, reporting basis, and as-of date. Separate a global operating market from a regional investable universe when both matter. State whether market size means revenue, shipments, installed base, transaction value, or another measure.

### 2. Explain how the sector works

Map the value chain from inputs to distribution and end customer. For each important layer, explain who pays, what is sold, how pricing is set, the main variable and fixed costs, working-capital behavior, capital intensity, switching costs, and the KPI that best exposes health. Identify where profits and bargaining power accrue; do not assume the largest revenue pool is the most attractive profit pool.

### 3. Separate structural and cyclical forces

Organize drivers into secular demand, penetration or mix, capacity and supply, pricing, regulation, technology, macro transmission, and industry cycle. For each driver, specify the observable indicator, direction of impact, lag, affected subsectors, and what would falsify the view. Distinguish temporary volume/pricing effects from durable changes in market structure.

### 4. Build the sector dashboard

Collect a small set of decision-relevant series before expanding coverage. Prefer consistent historical time series over a large collection of point estimates. Show current level, growth, trend, and dispersion where useful. For every derived statistic record the numerator, denominator, period, currency/unit, weighting, and source.

### 5. Construct and benchmark the peer universe

Start from economic similarity, not ticker familiarity. Tag each peer as leader, challenger, niche, platform/infrastructure, or adjacent substitute. Record geography, subsector exposure, end markets, fiscal year-end, reporting currency, and reasons for inclusion or exclusion. Use comparable columns for size, growth, profitability, returns, cash conversion, leverage, and valuation; separate non-comparable metrics rather than forcing them into the table.

### 6. Synthesize the investor debate

Turn the evidence into a few competing explanations of sector outcomes. For each, state what the market may be pricing, which data would support or disprove it, which companies are most sensitive, and the time horizon. Include a base view only when the evidence supports one; otherwise present the uncertainty and the leading indicators that can resolve it.

### 7. Publish and QA

Use the structure in [evidence and synthesis](evidence-and-synthesis.md). Check that every material table has a date and source, calculations reconcile to inputs, peer rows use like-for-like definitions, and narrative claims do not outrun the data. For persistent work, validate the workspace before saying the primer is reusable.

## Update discipline

On an update, compare the new source or observation with the prior record. Preserve both dates, mark the old record superseded, and state whether the change affects sector structure, cycle assessment, dashboard values, comp ranking, or only wording. Do not refresh every section merely because one peer reported a quarter.
